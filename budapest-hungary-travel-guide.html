FIRST, UNDERSTAND THE CITY

Budapest is not one place with a river running through it. Buda, on the west bank, is hilly, quieter and built for views. Pest, on the east bank, is flat, busy and where most of the restaurants, nightlife and everyday city energy live. They were separate cities until 1873, and they still feel like it.

For a first visit, sleep in Pest and spend at least half a day walking Buda. Three full days works. Four lets the city breathe.

WHAT IS ACTUALLY WORTH YOUR TIME

Parliament and the Danube edge

The Hungarian Parliament Building earns its reputation. See it from across the river in Buda, then walk the Pest bank past the Shoes on the Danube memorial. If you want to go inside Parliament, reserve an official timed visit rather than hoping for a same-day opening.

Castle Hill before the buses

Cross the Chain Bridge and climb or take transit to Castle Hill early. Fisherman's Bastion is theatrical, Matthias Church is the real architectural anchor, and the lanes behind them are better once you leave the main viewpoint.

The Jewish Quarter beyond the ruin bars

District VII is not merely nightlife. The Dohány Street Synagogue, smaller synagogues, courtyards and memorials tell a difficult, essential part of the city's history. The ruin bars came later. See both layers.

Andrássy Avenue to City Park

Walk or ride the historic M1 metro beneath Andrássy Avenue. The Opera House, Heroes' Square and City Park form one clean route, with Széchenyi Thermal Bath waiting at the far end.

A bath, not a bath tour

Pick one and stay long enough to understand it. Széchenyi is vast, social and visually unmistakable. Rudas is older, more atmospheric and has its own schedule rules. Lukács is less theatrical and more local.

Current bath note · 2026

Gellért Thermal Bath closed for renovation on October 1, 2025 and is expected to reopen in 2028. Do not build a current itinerary around it. Use Széchenyi, Rudas or Lukács instead. Rudas does not admit children under 14 and its Turkish bath schedule changes between men-only, women-only and co-ed periods, so check the official schedule before going.

WHAT TO EAT

  • Gulyás: the Hungarian original is a paprika-rich soup, not the thick stew many visitors expect.
  • Pörkölt: the slow-cooked meat dish closer to what outsiders often call goulash.
  • Chicken paprikash: paprika, chicken and sour cream, ideally with nokedli dumplings.
  • Lángos: fried dough usually topped with garlic, sour cream and cheese. Share one before pretending it is a snack.
  • Kürtőskalács: chimney cake cooked around a cylinder and coated with sugar.
  • Rétes: Hungarian strudel, often filled with sour cherry, apple, poppy seed or sweet cheese.

Eat at a market, but do not confuse the Great Market Hall's most obvious upstairs stalls with the full range of Hungarian cooking. Mix a traditional lunch with a modern Hungarian dinner and one old coffee house. Check the bill before adding a tip; many restaurants already include a service charge.

THREE DAYS THAT MAKE SENSE

DAY 01

Pest and the river

Parliament, Shoes on the Danube, St. Stephen's Basilica, the Jewish Quarter and an evening river view.

DAY 02

Buda and the baths

Castle Hill early, Matthias Church, Fisherman's Bastion, a walk down through Buda, then Rudas or Széchenyi.

DAY 03

The city's long axis

Central Market Hall, Andrássy Avenue, the Opera, M1 to City Park, then a proper Hungarian dinner.

GETTING AROUND WITHOUT MAKING IT HARD

Budapest has excellent metro, tram, bus and trolleybus coverage. Tram 2 along the Pest riverbank and trams 4 and 6 around the central ring are useful sightseeing tools disguised as public transport. The official BudapestGO app handles route planning and digital tickets.

Validate single tickets. Buying one is not enough. Paper singles must be stamped or punched; digital tickets require the appropriate code or NFC validation. Daily travelcards and passes do not require validation. The 100E Airport Express uses its own airport ticket, not a normal single ticket.

The Budapest Card can make sense if you will use the included museums, Lukács Bath, transport and other attractions. Do the arithmetic against your actual plan; unlimited transit by itself does not automatically justify the city card.

MONEY, TIMING AND SMALL TRAPS

  • Currency: Hungary uses the forint, not the euro. If a terminal offers currency conversion, choose to pay in HUF.
  • Cards: widely accepted, but carry some cash for small purchases and markets.
  • Best timing: April through June and September through October balance weather and crowds. Summer is lively and busy; winter is cold but atmospheric.
  • Taxis: use a licensed operator or established app. Do not accept a random offer outside the airport or station.
  • Bath gear: bring a swimsuit and sandals. Check whether your ticket includes a locker or cabin.
  • Nightlife: District VII is fun. It is also residential. Choose a quieter side street if sleep matters.
