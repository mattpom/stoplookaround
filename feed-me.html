HOW THIS WORKS

Every place on this page was either visited by someone in the StopLookAround community or comes from sources we trust. No paid placements. No sponsored restaurants. No "best of" lists assembled by people who've never left the office.

The rule is simple: no faces, just places — and that applies to food too. We're not here to photograph ourselves eating. We're here to tell you where to go so you can eat the thing and remember it forever.

Under $10

CHEAP EATS WORTH FINDING

$ Cheap
📍 Bangkok, Thailand

Pad Thai from a Street Cart

The ones that cost 60 baht (~$1.70) from a cart with no English menu are always better than the ones in restaurants. Look for the cart with the line of locals at 7pm. Sit on the plastic stool. Eat it hot.

~$2 Street Food
📍 Istanbul, Turkey

Simit from a Street Vendor

A sesame-crusted bread ring sold from carts all over the city for about 10 lira. Eat it while walking along the Bosphorus at sunrise. Breakfast has never cost less or felt more right.

~$0.50 Street Food
📍 Rome, Italy

Pizza al Taglio

Pizza sold by the slice, by weight, from a counter. The good places have it displayed in long rectangular trays. You point, they cut, they weigh it. Lunch for €2.50. Romans eat standing up. Do the same.

~$3 Local Staple
📍 Hanoi, Vietnam

Pho from a Sidewalk Stall

The plastic chairs are low to the ground. The broth has been simmering since before you woke up. A bowl costs 40,000 dong. It will be one of the best things you eat anywhere. Order the beef.

~$1.75 Street Food
📍 Mexico City, Mexico

Tacos al Pastor

The vertical spit with pork and pineapple, carved to order, served on a doubled-up corn tortilla with cilantro and onion. Three tacos for $2. The taqueria at the corner at midnight is always the right answer.

~$2 Street Food
📍 Singapore

Hawker Centre Anything

Singapore has Michelin-starred hawker stalls. The famous Liao Fan Chicken Rice costs $3. Every hawker centre has something extraordinary if you walk the whole thing before deciding. Never order from the first stall you see.

~$3–$5 Hawker Centre
$10–$50

SIT DOWN. STAY A WHILE.

$$ Mid
📍 San Sebastián, Spain

Pintxos Bar Crawl

The Basque Country's answer to tapas. Small bites on bread, displayed on bar counters. Walk from bar to bar in the old town, eat two or three at each, drink a small glass of txakoli. Dinner for €20 that takes three hours.

~$25 Bar Food
📍 Marrakech, Morocco

Tagine at a Local Restaurant

Not at the restaurants on Djemaa el-Fna. Walk three blocks into the medina. Find a place with a handwritten menu and plastic tablecloths. Order the lamb tagine with preserved lemon and olives. Take your time.

~$8 Sit Down
📍 Tokyo, Japan

Ramen at a Counter

Buy your ticket from the vending machine. Sit at the counter. The chef doesn't speak English. Point at the ticket. Eat in something approaching reverent silence. The tonkotsu broth took 20 hours to make.

~$10 Counter Dining
📍 Lisbon, Portugal

Lunch Menu (Prato do Dia)

The daily special at any neighborhood restaurant: soup, main, bread, wine, and dessert for €10–12. The restaurants don't advertise this to tourists. Ask for the menu do dia. It changes every day and it's always good.

~$12 Daily Special
📍 Buenos Aires, Argentina

Asado at a Parrilla

Argentina takes beef seriously in the way other countries take art seriously. A proper parrilla with chimichurri and a glass of Malbec costs $15 and lasts two hours. Do not rush this. It cannot be rushed.

~$15 Sit Down
📍 Naples, Italy

Pizza Napoletana

Naples invented pizza and hasn't stopped perfecting it. The crust chars at the edges in a wood-fired oven in 90 seconds. A margherita at a proper pizzeria costs €5. Eating it anywhere else in the world is now a compromise.

~$6 Local Classic
Worth Every Penny

THE OCCASIONAL SPLURGE

$$$ Splurge
📍 Lima, Peru

Central or Maido

Two of the world's top-ranked restaurants, both in Lima. Central serves ingredients organized by altitude — from the ocean floor to the Andes. Maido is Nikkei cuisine (Japanese-Peruvian). Book months ahead. Worth the planning.

~$150–$200 World Class
📍 Copenhagen, Denmark

Noma (or Its Successors)

Noma closed in 2024 but its influence on Nordic cuisine means Copenhagen is still the best food city in Scandinavia. The chefs who trained there opened their own places. Research before you go. Book before you land.

~$200+ Destination Dining
📍 Kyoto, Japan

Kaiseki Dinner

A multi-course Japanese dinner built around seasonality, technique, and restraint. Each dish is a small artwork. The meal takes three hours. You will remember it for the rest of your life. Some things are worth spending money on.

~$100–$300 Special Occasion
Go Early

MARKETS WORTH THE MORNING

$ Markets
📍 Barcelona, Spain

La Boqueria

Go before 9am when it's a working market, not a tourist attraction. The fruit vendors in the back sell the best produce in the city. The jamón ibérico sliced to order at counter stalls. Avoid the overpriced stalls at the entrance.

Free entry Food Market
📍 Istanbul, Turkey

Grand Bazaar & Spice Market

The Spice Market (Mısır Çarşısı) is where locals actually buy spices, dried fruits, and Turkish delight. The Grand Bazaar is for everything else. Go to the Spice Market for food. Negotiate. Try before you buy.

Free entry Spice Market
📍 Marrakech, Morocco

Mellah Market

The Jewish quarter's market — less touristy than the main souks. Olives, preserved lemons, spices, dried fruits, and fresh bread sold by weight. Get there at 8am and watch the city wake up around you.

Free entry Local Market
📍 Tokyo, Japan

Tsukiji Outer Market

The inner market moved to Toyosu but the outer market remains and is still extraordinary. Fresh sushi for breakfast at 7am from stalls that have been doing this for decades. Bring cash. Get there early.

~$5–$15 Fish Market
📍 Mexico City, Mexico

Mercado de Jamaica

The flower market that also sells food. Pozole, tamales, and aguas frescas from vendors who've been here since before you were born. The city comes here for flowers and leaves having eaten. Follow their lead.

~$3–$6 Market Food
📍 Chiang Mai, Thailand

Saturday Walking Street

Every Saturday evening, Wualai Road closes to traffic and fills with food vendors, artisans, and locals. Northern Thai food — khao soi, sai oua sausage, sticky rice with mango — that you won't find in Bangkok.

~$2–$5 Night Market
