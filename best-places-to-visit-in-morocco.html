Morocco Travel Guide

BEST PLACES
TO VISIT IN MOROCCO

Morocco combines ancient imperial cities, the High Atlas mountains, Sahara desert landscapes, and an Atlantic coastline. Here's how to approach it.

THE FOUR MOROCCOS

Morocco contains several distinct environments within one country. You can move from historic medinas to mountain landscapes, desert regions, and Atlantic towns in a single trip. Most visitors focus on one or two; this guide gives you a framework for seeing more.

THE IMPERIAL CITIES

Fes — The Medieval City

Fes el-Bali is a dense historic medina where a local guide can be useful for orientation. Once you understand the layout, leave time to wander rather than treating every alley as a checklist.

Marrakech — Navigate It, Don't Get Navigated

Djemaa el-Fna at sunset and the surrounding souks are central to the Marrakech experience. Agree on prices before purchases or services and approach the medina with patience rather than a rigid schedule.

Chefchaouen — The Blue City

This Rif mountain town is known for blue-painted buildings and steep lanes. The walk toward the Spanish Mosque above town provides a broad view back over Chefchaouen.

THE DESERT

Merzouga is a long road journey from Marrakech, so build time into the route rather than treating the desert as a quick side trip. The Draa Valley, kasbahs, oases, and gorges are part of the experience.

PRACTICAL NOTES

TopicWhat You Need to Know
CurrencyMoroccan dirham. Check current exchange and currency rules before travel.
NegotiatingBargaining is common in many souks. Keep it friendly and decide what the item is worth to you.
DressDress expectations vary by setting. More modest clothing can be appropriate at religious and traditional sites.
FoodTagine, couscous, pastilla, and harira are widely associated with Moroccan cooking. Restaurants away from the busiest visitor zones can offer a different experience.
SafetyUse ordinary travel precautions and consult current official travel guidance before departure. Tourist areas can also involve persistent sales approaches.

Morocco rewards travelers who leave enough time for the medinas, landscapes, food, and long distances between them.
