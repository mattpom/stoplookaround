Astronomy · North America

TOTALITY

On April 8, 2024, a total solar eclipse crossed a wide section of North America, giving millions of people a rare chance to watch the Moon completely cover the Sun.

THE PATH OF TOTALITY

The path of totality stretched from Mexico through the United States and into Canada. In the U.S., it passed through parts of Texas, Arkansas, Missouri, Illinois, Indiana, Ohio, Pennsylvania, New York, Vermont, New Hampshire and Maine.

WHEN THE SKY WENT DARK

For people inside that path, the experience was far more dramatic than simply watching the Sun disappear. Temperatures dropped. The sky darkened in the middle of the day. Bright planets and stars became visible. Birds and other animals sometimes reacted as if evening had suddenly arrived.

THE CORONA APPEARS

During totality, the Sun's outer atmosphere -- the corona -- became visible as a pale, glowing halo surrounding the black disk of the Moon. It is one of the few times people can see the corona with the naked eye.

Some experiences are impressive in photographs. Totality is one you really have to see.
WHY IT FELT DIFFERENT THIS TIME

What made the April 8 eclipse especially memorable was its accessibility. Major cities were located directly in or near the path, and some locations experienced more than four minutes of totality.

PREDICTABLE, YET IMPOSSIBLE TO DESCRIBE

A total solar eclipse is both predictable and strangely difficult to describe. We know exactly why it happens: the Moon passes directly between Earth and the Sun. But standing beneath one feels much less ordinary.

For a few minutes on April 8, 2024, daylight disappeared, the horizon changed, and millions of people simply stopped what they were doing and looked up.
