Been There, Done That · Paris

THE THREE BEST THINGS TO DO IN PARIS

Not a frantic checklist. Three experiences that reveal the city from the water, through its art and along the streets where Paris still feels like a place instead of a backdrop.

Paris punishes people who try to conquer it. The city works better when you choose less, walk more and leave space between the landmarks. For a first visit, these are the three experiences worth protecting time for.

1

SEE PARIS FROM THE SEINE

The Seine is the city's organizing line. From its banks, Paris stops feeling like disconnected monuments and begins to make geographical sense: the Eiffel Tower upriver, the Louvre beside the water, the Île de la Cité at the historic center and bridges stitching the two banks together.

Walk a long section near sunset if the weather cooperates. A river cruise is not a substitute for exploring on foot, but an evening trip can show the facades and bridges from the angle for which many were designed. Do both if time allows: walk first, then let the river replay the city after dark.

Do it well: Skip the rushed daytime photo stop. Begin around the Tuileries or Île de la Cité and follow the river without treating every bridge as another box to check.
2

STEP INSIDE THE LOUVRE

The Louvre is too large to “do.” That is the first thing to accept. Its collections stretch across civilizations and centuries inside a former royal palace, and the building itself is part of the experience.

Choose one wing, period or group of rooms before you enter. See the Mona Lisa if it matters to you, then keep going. The museum becomes far more rewarding once the crowd thins and you can spend ten quiet minutes with an object you did not arrive intending to find.

Do it well: Reserve through the Louvre's official website, check the current entrance instructions and arrive with a short priority list—not an impossible plan to see everything.
3

WANDER MONTMARTRE EARLY

Montmartre has two personalities. Around midday, the streets near Sacré-Cœur and Place du Tertre can feel like a moving crowd. Early in the morning, the hill reveals what makes it worth visiting: steep lanes, stairways, neighborhood cafés, old facades and sudden views across Paris.

Start high and wander down rather than following one prescribed route. Give the quieter residential streets as much attention as the basilica. The point is not to reproduce somebody else's photograph; it is to notice how the neighborhood changes block by block.

Do it well: Arrive early, wear shoes made for hills and keep your route loose. Montmartre is better discovered than completed.
The best first day in Paris is not the day you see the most. It is the day the city stops feeling like a list.

Plan with current official information from the Louvre and Paris je t'aime, the city's official tourism office. Hours, reservations and access conditions can change.
