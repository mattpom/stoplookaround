History · Paris, France

POINT ZÉRO

Everyone looks up at Notre-Dame. The small marker beneath their feet quietly tells France where every road from Paris begins.

THE LANDMARK MOST PEOPLE STEP OVER

In the square outside Notre-Dame, set into the paving stones, is a small circular marker divided into four sections around an octagonal compass rose. Its full name is Point Zéro des Routes de France—Point Zero of the Roads of France.

It is the reference point used to calculate road distances between Paris and other French cities. That makes it Paris's kilometer zero, not the geographic center of France. Its power is practical and symbolic: the road network is imagined as radiating outward from the capital.

Paris has monuments built to be seen from miles away. Point Zéro matters because it is almost invisible.

WHY NOTRE-DAME?

The location is not random. The Île de la Cité is the historic heart of Paris, and the cathedral's forecourt has served as a civic stage for centuries. According to the City of Paris, a medieval justice post once stood in this area. A triangular stone was installed here as a road reference in 1768.

The familiar circular plaque—with its brass compass rose and four directional motifs—dates to 1924. It compresses a large national idea into something only about thirty centimeters across: Paris as the point from which the country's roads are measured.

THE ORIGINAL DID NOT SURVIVE UNCHANGED

After the Notre-Dame fire in April 2019, the medallion was removed because it had been contaminated with lead. The City initially hoped to clean and restore it, but the damage proved too severe. Municipal metalworkers made an identical replacement, which was installed on July 1, 2025. The original was transferred to the Musée Carnavalet for preservation.

What visitors see today: a faithful replacement of the historic 1924 marker, set into granite edging on the Parvis Notre-Dame.

THE PROMISE UNDERFOOT

A modern bit of Paris folklore says that stepping on the marker means you will return to the city. There is no official ceremony and no rulebook. That is exactly why it works. Travelers turn a piece of road infrastructure into a private promise: this visit will not be the last.

To find it, walk onto the Parvis Notre-Dame and look down in front of the cathedral. The challenge is not reaching Point Zéro. It is remembering to stop looking up.
